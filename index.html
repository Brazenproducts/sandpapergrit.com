Sandpaper Grit Guide 2026

Updated 2026

Grit is the single most important number on a sheet of sandpaper — it decides whether you strip material fast, smooth a surface, or ruin a finish. This guide breaks down which grit to reach for and why.

Sandpaper Grit Chart

Grit RangeCategoryBest For
40–60Extra CoarseStripping paint, heavy material removal, rough shaping
80–120Coarse / MediumSmoothing rough wood, removing old finish, first sanding pass
150–180Medium / FineFinal sanding before priming or staining
220–320FineBetween-coat sanding, light finishing
400–600Very FineWet sanding, polishing, metal and auto work
800–2000+Ultra FineFinal polishing, lacquer, clear-coat finishing

How to Pick the Right Grit

  • Start coarse, finish fine. Work up through grits in steps — skipping too many leaves scratches the next grit can't erase.
  • Don't jump more than ~100 grit at a time in the lower range. 80 → 120 → 180 → 220 is a reliable progression.
  • Match the material. Softwoods clog fine grits fast; metal and auto paint want very fine, wet-sand-friendly grits.
  • Higher grit = smoother, not faster. Coarse grit removes material; fine grit refines what's already there.

Common Mistakes

Using one grit for everything. A single mid grit leaves heavy work slow and fine work scratched. Keep a small range on hand.

Over-sanding with fine grit. Going too fine too early just burnishes the surface and stops stain or paint from adhering.
